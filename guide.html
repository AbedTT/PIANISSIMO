Getting started

Open the editor. A blue box, the cursor, sits on the first beat. Everything you type happens at the cursor, and the cursor moves on by itself.

Click New to choose an instrument (piano, voice + piano, lead sheet, violin, …), a key, a time signature and a tempo. You can change all of them later in the Score setup panel on the right.

Stuck? Press ? in the editor for every shortcut, or Ctrl+Shift+P to search for any command by name.

Writing notes

  1. Choose a length: 1 whole, 2 half, 3 quarter, 4 eighth, 5 16th, 6 32nd, 7 64th. . makes it dotted.
  2. Type the note with A–G. Pianissimo picks the octave closest to the previous note, so melodies flow naturally.
  3. Keep typing. When a bar is full the cursor moves into the next one, and a new bar is added at the end when you need it.

A note too long for the space left in the bar is split across the bar line and tied, so a rhythm is never cut short. You can also press I and click on the staff to place notes with the mouse.

Chords: Shift+A–G adds a note above the last one; Alt+2…8 adds an interval (a third is Alt+3).

MIDI keyboard: click 🎹 MIDI (or Shift+M) and play; keys pressed together become a chord. Works in Chrome and Edge.

Changing notes

  • ↑ ↓ move a note by step; Ctrl+↑ ↓ by octave; Alt+Shift+↑ ↓ by semitone.
  • = sharp, - flat, N natural (press again to undo); J respells (C♯ ↔ D♭).
  • Move onto a note with ← → and press a length key to change its duration.
  • Delete turns a note into a rest; Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo.
  • Alt+T transposes the whole score (key signature and chord symbols too) or just the selection.

Rests, ties, triplets

0 writes a rest. , ties a note to the next one. Shift+3 switches triplet input on or off: three triplet eighths fill one beat. S adds a slur to the next note, or over the selected notes. Leftover rests are tidied automatically into clean, beat-aligned values.

Up to four voices on one staff

Press V to write another independent line on the same staff: a held melody over moving inner notes, or soprano and alto together. V goes through voices 1, 2, 3 and 4. Voices 1 and 3 have stems up, 2 and 4 stems down, and each has its own cursor colour. Ctrl+Shift+V removes the voice you are on from the bar.

Selection & clipboard

Hold Shift with the arrow keys (or Shift+click) to select. Ctrl+Shift+→ selects bar by bar; Ctrl+A selects everything. Then Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V, Delete, or transpose with the arrow keys.

Articulations & dynamics

Press Tab to switch the number row: in the Articulations tab 1–5 are staccato, accent, tenuto, marcato and fermata; in the Dynamics tab 1–9 are ppp … sfz. Esc returns to the notes tab. Dynamics and accents also change the playback volume.

Lyrics & verses

Put the cursor on the first note and press Ctrl+L. Type a syllable, then:

Chord symbols & backing

Ctrl+K types chord symbols above the staff: C, Am7, F#m7b5, Bbmaj7, G/B… Space moves to the next note or rest.

Turn on Chord accompaniment (Alt+A, or in the side panel) and the chords play as a backing band in one of five styles, with its own instrument.

Bars, repeats & jumps

  • [ and ] start and end a repeat; Ctrl+] marks 1st / 2nd endings.
  • The 𝄋 menu on the toolbar adds Segno, Coda, Fine, To Coda, D.C. and D.S. (al Fine / al Coda). Playback follows every jump.
  • Alt+Enter inserts a bar; Ctrl+Backspace deletes one.

Sounds & playback

Space plays from the cursor's bar, Ctrl+Space from the start. With notes selected, Space plays just the selection; L loops it. Alt+M turns the metronome on, Alt+C adds a one-bar count-in. Choose each staff's instrument under Sounds.

Layout & printing

Ctrl+P opens the page preview: choose A4 or US Letter and a staff size, then Print / Save PDF and pick Save as PDF for a file. Ctrl+Enter forces a new line after a bar; Ctrl+Alt+Enter starts a new page.

Saving, files & sharing

  • Autosave: the score you are working on is kept in this browser.
  • Your library: sign in (free) and Ctrl+S saves to My scores, so you can reopen them on any computer.
  • Share: make a score unlisted (only people with the link) or public (listed on Explore). The link opens a page where anyone can see and play it.
  • Files: open and export MusicXML (.musicxml, .mxl), so you can work with MuseScore, Flat, Finale, Sibelius and Dorico. You can also export MIDI and download a .pianissimo.json backup.

Guitar and drums

Choose Guitar + tab in Instruments… (or press Alt+Shift+B on any staff) for tablature worked out from the notes in standard tuning. Alt+Shift+D draws chord diagrams above the chord symbols. A Drum kit part has one drum per line or space: kick, snare, toms, hi-hat, ride and crash; the status bar names the drums under the cursor.

Mixer and practice

In the side panel, Mixer & practice sets the volume of each part with mute and solo, the reverb and swing. Practice tempo slows playback down (or speeds it up) without changing the written tempo. Wait mode (Ctrl+Alt+W) stops on each chord of your part until you play it: on a MIDI keyboard, the on-screen piano, or by singing into the microphone.

Microphone and tuner

Ctrl+Alt+R (🎤) counts you in, then listens: sing or play one line and it is written as notes. Ctrl+Alt+U opens a tuner that shows the note, how many cents off it is, and turns green when you are in tune. The sound stays in your browser.

Writing together

In Share, invite people by their account email: the score appears in their library. When you both have it open you edit at the same time and see each other’s cursors; Ctrl+Z only takes back your own edits. Comment on a bar with 💬 (Ctrl+Alt+M), and use Version history to go back to an earlier save.

Classes and assignments

📚 Classes (Ctrl+Alt+J): a teacher creates a class and shares its code or link, then sets the score they are editing as an assignment, with instructions, a due date and marking criteria. Students start it (they get their own copy), hand it in, and see the feedback, grade and marks on top of their score. The due dates can be added to any calendar app.

Scanning sheet music

Ctrl+Alt+O turns a photo or PDF of printed music into an editable score, when the site has a scanning engine set up. Use a straight, sharp photo of one page; then check the notes against the page and fix what the scanner misread.

Offline and on your phone

Install Pianissimo from your browser’s menu (Install app or Add to Home Screen): it opens like an app and works without a connection; your library saves again when you are back online. On a touch screen, the on-screen piano (Shift+K) writes notes with a tap.
