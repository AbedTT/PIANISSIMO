Privacy policy

Plain-language summary of what Pianissimo stores and why.

Without an account

The editor works entirely in your browser. The score you are editing is saved in your browser's local storage on your own device. It is not sent to us.

Instrument sounds are downloaded from public sample libraries the first time you use them (GitHub Pages hosts the Salamander piano and FluidR3 soundfont files). Like any website, those hosts can see your IP address when your browser requests the files.

With an account

If you create an account we store:

  • your name and email address, to identify you and let you sign in;
  • your password, only as a one-way hash (we can never read it);
  • the scores you save to your library, with their title and sharing setting;
  • a short record of sign-in attempts per IP address, to slow down password guessing (kept for about an hour).

Sharing

Scores are private by default. If you make a score unlisted, anyone with its link can view and play it. If you make it public, it is also listed on the Explore page with your display name. You can make it private again at any time.

Cookies

We use a single session cookie to keep you signed in. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies, or third-party analytics.

Your rights

You can delete any score from your library at any time. To delete your account and all its data, contact the site operator.

This page describes how the Pianissimo software works. Whoever runs a Pianissimo site should review it and add their contact details and any legal requirements that apply to them.
